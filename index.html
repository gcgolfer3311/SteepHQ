FAQ

Common tea questions, answered straight

How long should I steep green tea?

1–3 minutes at 70–80°C. Green tea is the most forgiving-to-ruin category — oversteeping past a few minutes at high heat is what causes the bitterness most people associate with "bad" green tea. Use the steep timer to get it right every time.

Is pu-erh tea caffeinated?

Yes — pu-erh comes from the same Camellia sinensis plant as every other tea on this site, so it contains caffeine. Fermentation changes flavor, not caffeine content in any significant way.

What's the difference between oolong and black tea?

Oxidation level. Oolong is partially oxidized (10–80%, the widest range of any category), while black tea is fully oxidized (~100%). See the full breakdown on the tea encyclopedia.

Is ceremonial-grade matcha worth the extra price?

Usually yes for drinking straight, but the bigger issue in 2026 is mislabeling, not just price — a lot of "ceremonial grade" on shelves right now isn't genuine. Run our free matcha authenticity checker before you pay a premium.

What's the best tea for a beginner?

A mid-oxidation oolong or a straightforward black tea like Assam is the easiest entry point — more forgiving on brew time and temperature than delicate greens or whites. See our independent reviews for specific picks.

How much caffeine is in your tea?

Most charts online give one confident number per tea type. That's misleading — caffeine varies enormously with leaf, harvest, water temperature, steep time, and how much leaf you use. These are honest ranges per 8oz cup, not false precision.

Ranges are typical for an 8oz cup at standard brewing parameters. Your actual cup can fall outside them — a heavily-leafed gongfu session or a long steep will push higher, a quick rinse-and-pour lower.
